# Retention of Critical Knowledge: Beth Collier – Environmental Specialist

**Report Number:** SRN-010382
**Author(s):** Mark Kosiara, Beth B Collier

## General Background and Executive Summary

This report summarizes the Retention of Critical Knowledge (ROCK) exercise conducted for Beth Collier, an Environmental Specialist retiring from Shell after 21 years of service. Ms. Collier's expertise lies in managing air, water, and waste environmental categories at the Shell Technology Center Houston (STCH). The ROCK exercise aimed to capture her extensive knowledge of regulatory jurisdictions, reporting requirements, historical environmental information, key lessons learned, best practices, risks/challenges, and recommendations for improvement. The report identifies several best practices, lessons learned, risks, and opportunities for improvement.

## Profile Competency and Tacit Knowledge

Ms. Collier's competency includes extensive knowledge of:

*   Regulatory frameworks for air, water, and waste management (TCEQ, EPA).
*   Permitting processes for new sources (Title 5, PBRs).
*   Reporting requirements (Deviation Report, Air Emissions Inventory, Texas Tier 2, etc.).
*   Waste management processes (hazardous and non-hazardous waste).
*   Stakeholder management (internal Shell SMEs, external regulatory contacts).
*   Sustainability initiatives and their implementation challenges.

Her tacit knowledge includes understanding the nuances of site-specific environmental issues (MB Tank Farm contamination, holding ponds), the importance of personal relationships with stakeholders, and effective communication strategies for environmental support.

## Risks and Challenges for the Future

The following risks and challenges were identified:

*   Potential complications in environmental permits and compliance if STCH moves to the Woodcreek site or undergoes significant changes in operations.
*   The risk of increased regulatory scrutiny due to numerous small environmental permit violations.
*   Difficulties in implementing Shell Group sustainability goals at a research facility like STCH due to misaligned opportunities and limited influence of environmental staff.
*   Maintaining the holding ponds and managing litter to prevent environmental issues.

## Replicable Content and Potential Values

### Best Practices

*   **Effective Environmental Training:** In-person training for staff and contractors is more effective in ensuring compliance with storm water discharge limits.
*   **Centralized Compliance Tracking:** Managing all environmental compliance requirements in a single electronic repository (e.g., Excel spreadsheet) improves oversight and reduces non-compliance risks.
*   **Effective Communication:** Open and honest communication, coupled with strong interpersonal skills, is critical for environmental support staff, especially when dealing with regulators and other stakeholders.

### Lessons Learned

*   **Post-Incident Site Inspections:** Site environmental experts should inspect site conditions after an environmental incident to understand the causes and prevent recurrence.
*   **Accountability for Sample Spills:** Holding shipping parties accountable for improper packaging significantly reduces sample spill volumes.

### Opportunities for Improvement

*   Develop and implement a sandblasting procedure to protect neighboring properties from potential impacts.
*   Revisit and potentially incorporate the Wildlife at Work program to enhance Respect for Nature activities.

## Recommendations

*   Maintain strong personal relationships with key site stakeholders to stay informed of changes and ensure seamless handover of activities.
*   Develop a sandblasting procedure for the new sandblasting area to protect neighboring properties in certain wind conditions.
