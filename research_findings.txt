**Retention of Critical Knowledge - Beth Collier – Environmental Specialist: Air, Water and Waste - GSUSI-PTS-S**

*Report Number:* SRN-010382
*Author(s):* Kosiara, Mark; Collier, Beth B

**General Background:**

This ROCK exercise was conducted for Beth Collier, who was retiring after 21 years of service at Shell. The aim was to capture Ms. Collier’s extensive experience managing air, water, and waste at the Shell Technology Center Houston (STCH) to supplement handover activities to her team lead, Rob Smith, and her replacement, Maurice Pinder. The ROCK aimed to inventory regulatory jurisdictions, reporting requirements, historical environmental information, key lessons learned, best practices, future risks/challenges, and recommendations.

**Profile Competency and Tacit Knowledge:**

Ms. Collier's core competency lies in environmental management, specifically air, water, and waste within HSSE. Her responsibilities included record keeping, reporting to entities like TCEQ and Shell Group, ensuring compliance with permits and regulations, and providing environmental support for site operations and projects.

**Risks and Challenges for the Future:**

1.  **Relocation of STCH:** Moving STCH to the Woodcreek site poses challenges, including the need to analyze and acquire new permits, potentially impacting grandfathered permits, and managing environmental compliance during the transition.
2.  **Changing Activities at STCH:** Uncertainty around STCH’s future (relocation, downsizing, or expansion with third parties) could complicate environmental permits and compliance.
3.  **Minor Permit Violations:** Numerous small, overlooked permit violations can attract unwanted regulatory attention and lead to inspections.
4.  **Sustainability Goals:** Implementing Shell Group’s sustainability goals at a research facility like STCH is difficult due to misalignment with site opportunities, limited influence of environmental staff, and budget constraints.

**Replicable Content and Potential Values:**

*   **Best Practices:**
    *   In-person environmental training for on-site staff improves storm water discharge compliance.
    *   Centralized tracking tool enhances environmental compliance requirement monitoring.
    *   Effective communication and interpersonal skills are critical for environmental support staff.
*   **Lessons Learned:**
    *   Site environmental experts should inspect site conditions after environmental incidents.
    *   Holding shipping parties accountable for improper packaging significantly reduces sample spills.
*   **Opportunities for Improvement:**
    *   Develop and implement a sandblasting procedure for the new sandblasting area to protect neighboring properties.
    *   Maintaining strong personal relationships with stakeholders to stay ahead of environmental changes.

**Recommendations:**

1.  Staying ahead of Environmental changes at a Shell laboratory have been dependent upon having strong personal relationships with key site stakeholders based upon mutual trust.
2.  A sandblasting procedure for the new sandblasting area at the Shell Technology Center Houston should be developed and implemented to protect neighbouring properties in certain wind conditions.
